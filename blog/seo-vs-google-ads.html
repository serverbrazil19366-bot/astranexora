SEO vs Google Ads: which should a small business start with?

SEO builds free traffic over time; Google Ads buys traffic today. How each works, what each costs you, and how to decide where to start.

S. Saktheeswaran28 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

Share

Key takeaways

  • Google Ads can bring enquiries quickly but stops the day you stop paying.
  • SEO takes months to build but keeps bringing visitors without paying per click.
  • Before either, make sure your website turns visitors into enquiries.
  • Many businesses use ads for quick results while SEO builds up, then rely more on SEO over time.

Every business owner who wants more enquiries from Google eventually asks the same question: should I pay for Google Ads, or invest in SEO? The honest answer is that they do different jobs, and the right starting point depends on your budget, your timeline and your website.

01How they differ

Google Ads puts your business at the top of the results for the searches you choose, and you pay each time someone clicks. You can start within days and control exactly which searches you appear for. When you stop paying, the traffic stops.

SEO (search engine optimisation) is the work of making your website rank in the free, organic results. It takes time, often several months, but the traffic it builds keeps coming without a cost per click.

02When to start with Google Ads

01

You need enquiries quickly

A new business, a new service or a seasonal offer cannot always wait months. Ads can bring targeted visitors from the first week.

02

You want to test what works

Ads show you quickly which searches and messages bring enquiries. Those lessons are valuable for your SEO and content later.

03

Your market is local and specific

For focused local searches, a well-built campaign with tight targeting can be efficient, as long as your landing page converts.

03When to start with SEO

04

You are building for the long term

If you plan to be in business for years, SEO builds an asset: pages that keep ranking and bringing visitors month after month.

05

Clicks in your industry are expensive

In some industries every click costs a lot. Ranking organically for those searches can be far better value over time.

06

People research before they buy

If customers read and compare before deciding, helpful content that ranks earns their trust before they ever contact you.

04The step most people skip

Neither SEO nor ads will help if your website loses visitors. Before spending on either, make sure your site loads fast on mobile, explains what you do clearly and makes contacting you effortless. Our website audit checklist walks through exactly what to check.

05A sensible plan for most small businesses

  1. Fix the website and set up enquiry tracking.
  2. Complete your Google Business Profile.
  3. Run a small, tightly targeted Google Ads campaign for quick enquiries and learning.
  4. Start SEO on the services and searches that matter most.
  5. As organic traffic grows, shift budget from ads to what is working best.

06Frequently asked questions

Is SEO free?

The clicks are free, but SEO takes time and skill: content, technical fixes and reputation building. You pay either in your own time or for someone to do it.

How much should I spend on Google Ads?

Start with a budget you can sustain for at least a month so the campaign has enough data to learn, and judge it on enquiries, not clicks.

Can I do both at the same time?

Yes, and many businesses do. Ads bring results now while SEO builds up; the data from ads also shows which keywords are worth ranking for.
