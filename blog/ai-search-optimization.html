How to show up in ChatGPT and Google AI answers: AEO and GEO explained

People now ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews instead of scrolling results. Here is what AEO and GEO mean and what you can do to be the answer.

S. Saktheeswaran28 Oct 2026 · 3 min read
On this page
  1. What AEO and GEO actually mean
  2. What you can do
  3. How to check where you stand
  4. Frequently asked questions
Share

Key takeaways

  • AEO and GEO mean making your content easy for AI tools to understand, quote and recommend.
  • AI tools lean on the same foundations as SEO: a crawlable site, clear pages and a trusted brand.
  • Answer real customer questions directly, in plain language, near the top of the page.
  • Consistent details about your business across the web help AI tools describe you correctly.

More and more people are asking questions in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews instead of clicking through ten blue links. The tool reads the web and writes a single answer. If your business is not part of that answer, you are invisible to those people.

That is what AEO (answer engine optimization) and GEO (generative engine optimization) are about: making your business easy for AI tools to find, understand and recommend.

01What AEO and GEO actually mean

The names are new, but the idea is simple. AI tools gather information from websites, reviews, directories and other sources, then summarise it. They prefer sources that are clear, specific, consistent and trusted. AEO and GEO are the work of making your content that kind of source.

They are not a replacement for SEO. Most AI tools use search indexes such as Google's or Bing's to find pages, so a site that is hard to crawl or poorly ranked is also less likely to be cited.

02What you can do

01

Make sure search engines can read your site

Submit your site to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. Bing matters more than people think, because several AI assistants use its index. Fix crawl errors and keep a current sitemap.

02

Answer questions directly

Write pages that answer the questions your customers actually ask, in plain language. Put the short answer first, then the detail. A clear question as a heading with a direct answer below is easy for both people and AI tools to use.

03

Be specific about who you are

State plainly what you do, who you serve, where you work and how to contact you, on your home page and about page. Vague slogans give AI tools nothing to work with.

04

Keep your details consistent everywhere

Your business name, description, services and contact details should match on your website, Google Business Profile, social profiles and directories. Conflicting information makes it harder for AI tools to describe you confidently.

05

Use structured data

Structured data (schema markup) labels your business details, services, articles and FAQs in a format machines read easily. It does not guarantee anything, but it removes guesswork.

06

Build real reputation

Reviews, mentions in local news or industry sites, and links from respected sources all signal that your business is trustworthy. AI tools tend to repeat what many trusted sources agree on.

03How to check where you stand

Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the questions your customers would ask, such as "best digital marketing agency for small businesses in Tamil Nadu" or a question about your service. Note which businesses are mentioned and which sources are cited. That shows you who you are competing with and what kind of content gets used.

04Frequently asked questions

Is AEO different from SEO?

It builds on SEO. The foundations are the same; AEO puts extra focus on direct answers, clear facts about your business and consistent information across the web.

Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT answers?

No. There is no paid placement in ChatGPT's organic answers. Visibility comes from being a clear, trusted source.

How long does it take to show up in AI answers?

It varies. Once your pages are indexed and your details are consistent, some tools pick changes up within weeks; building the reputation that gets you recommended takes longer.
